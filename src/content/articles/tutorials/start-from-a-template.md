---
title: Start from a template
---

Kolumn's real templates only exist inside your account for a moment — right after signup. After that, "start from a template" mostly means reusing something you already built. This tutorial covers that part: duplicating a card whose fields you got right, plus where the public gallery and your getting-started board fit into the picture.

## Step 1 — Know where the actual templates are

If you're newly signed up, the templates live in onboarding: pick a role, pick one of the starter boards offered for it, and Kolumn builds it for you — columns, cards, checklists, done. That only happens once. See [Create a board from a template](/support/create-a-board-from-a-template) for the full walkthrough, and [Your getting-started board](/support/your-getting-started-board) for the "Welcome" board that's seeded alongside it.

If you're past that step already, every new board from here starts blank — there's no "apply a template" option in the **New board** flow. Which is why the rest of this tutorial matters more day to day.

## Step 2 — Browse the gallery for ideas, not a shortcut

The [templates gallery](/templates) on Kolumn's site shows full board layouts you can look through before you've even signed up. Its **Use this template** button takes you into onboarding — but onboarding asks for your role and offers starter boards for that role, not necessarily the exact layout you were just looking at. Treat the gallery as a way to decide what kind of board to ask for, not a guarantee you'll get that literal layout.

## Step 3 — Duplicate a card instead of rebuilding one

This is the template tool you'll actually reach for once you're using Kolumn. Say you've got a card with the checklist, priority, and labels exactly right for a recurring kind of task — a bug report, a client onboarding step, whatever repeats. Open it, click the **⋯** menu, and choose **Duplicate**.

Kolumn creates a new card on the same board with the same title, description, priority, checklist (every item reset to unchecked), and labels. Rename it and you've got a fresh card shaped exactly like the one you liked, without retyping the checklist.

The same menu has a **Template** option, which saves a copy of the card's fields to your browser instead of duplicating it in place. Right now that's one-way — there's no picker yet to bring a saved one back onto a new card — so it's closer to a personal note of a layout you want to remember than a working template library. Duplicate is the one that actually gets you a usable card today.
