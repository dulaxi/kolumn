---
title: Chat vs the pill
---

Kolumn has two places to talk to the AI, and they don't do the same job. Mixing them up is the single most common point of confusion, so here's the exact split.

## The pill writes, and only on the board you're viewing

The pill sits at the bottom of every board. Type into it and the AI acts: it creates cards on Free, and on Pro it also moves, updates, completes, duplicates, and deletes cards, plus manages columns and the board itself. It's a doing surface, and everything it does is pinned to the one board you have open. See [What the pill can do](/support/what-the-pill-can-do).

## Chat reads across every board, and writes nothing

Chat is the standalone page at `/chat`. It's a conversation: ask something, get an answer. Chat can look things up — search your cards by text, pull the full detail on one card, or get a structured summary of a whole board — and it can do that across every board you belong to, not just one. What it cannot do, on Free or Pro, is create, move, update, complete, or delete anything. There is no write tool available to chat at all. See [Ask your boards a question](/tutorials/ask-your-boards-in-chat).

## Side by side

- **Where**: the pill lives on a board page; chat lives at `/chat`.
- **Scope**: the pill is locked to the current board; chat sees everything you're a member of.
- **Writes**: the pill can (create on Free, the full set on Pro); chat never can.
- **Reads**: chat can search and summarize on every plan, including Free; the pill doesn't do lookups at all — it just acts.

## One thing they share: the daily limit

On Free, a message to either surface counts against the same 20-a-day allowance. Asking chat a question and having the pill create a card both use one message. See [Why did the AI say I hit my daily limit?](/support/daily-limit).

## Why chat can't just do what the pill does

Chat's search and summary tools are built to look across all your boards at once — that's what makes "what's overdue this week" a useful question to ask. The pill is deliberately locked to a single board so a plain-language instruction typed on one board can never land on another one by mistake. Two different scopes, on purpose.
