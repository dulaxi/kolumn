A · Mark only — profile picture

The sprout is a 3-pixel-tall shape. It reads as a shape, not a symbol, so contrast against the field is everything — the ratio chips are computed live (WCAG relative luminance) for both the lime leaf and the olive stem.

C · Mauve fields — Klay's own colour

Mauve is Klay's pot hex, reserved in klayAnimations.js as m: '#A8969E' — KLAY ONLY, never props. These vary only the field; the sprout keeps its lime/olive. Note that the mauve family splits sharply — the two dark plums are the strongest fields on this whole page, while the light mauves are the weakest.

What the contrast numbers are telling you.
Lime #C2D64A on cream #F2EDE8 is about 1.4:1. That's fine in the sidebar, where the mark sits at 21px beside a wordmark that carries the meaning — but a profile picture is alone at 32px on someone else's timeline, with nothing to anchor it. On ink the same lime is about 10.8:1.

Olive fields are a trap. A #8BA32E field is the exact hex of the stem pixel, so the stem vanishes and the sprout loses its footing — the tile is flagged for that reason, not for taste.

Read the ΔE chip before the ratio chip. WCAG contrast is luminance-only and deliberately hue-blind — it was designed for text legibility, where hue can't be relied on. The sprout is a chunky colour block, not text, and against a mauve field almost all of its separation is carried by hue, which WCAG cannot measure at all.

That's why #A8969E scores a terrible 1.02:1 on the stem yet looks perfectly clear: its CIEDE2000 difference is ΔE 36 (leaf ΔE 40) — versus ΔE 27 for the cream field that ships as your favicon today. By the metric that matches the object, the pot hex outperforms the status quo. It holds up under deuteranopia too (ΔE 27/31), which is the one case where a hue-carried distinction genuinely degrades. The single real cost is greyscale rendering — in monochrome the stem does vanish.

The dark plums (#3A3040 ΔE 56, #2E2832) win on both metrics at once, which is the only reason to prefer them — not because mauve "fails."
